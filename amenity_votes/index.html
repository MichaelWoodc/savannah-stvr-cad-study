Definitions and limitations

A raw vote is one feature × focal parcel × call number. A call can vote for several features. Repeated listings do not duplicate the same feature/call vote inside one focal neighborhood. Shared neighbors can contribute to several rental neighborhoods, so totals are memberships, not regional unique-call totals. All calls are citizen-initiated, with patrol and proactive-stop dispatch labels excluded.

Expected calls = residential category calls per year × category years × exposure properties. Exposure properties are the rental booking fraction, the count of touching residential neighbors, or their sum. Subtract-average scoring uses observed minus expected counts, or annualized observed rate minus the residential reference. Simple signed votes retain only the sign. Normalizing uses the number of scored advertising properties, including zero-call advertisers.

Only established address/license matches and residential call coverage are used. Neighbors share a boundary, not just a corner or across-road proximity. Saved commercial/hotel exclusions remain. Unmeasured neighbors are omitted and audited. Missing parking coverage later in the study is not zero. Captured mentions and party language are screening evidence; they do not prove on-site availability or behavior. Many ordinary amenities occur at properties with unrelated calls.

Average subtraction and exposure normalization reduce obvious common-feature and neighborhood-size biases, but do not remove location, property type, reporting or other confounding. Negative scores are not proof an amenity prevents calls. Positive scores are not proof it causes calls. High-call properties are review priorities, not established offenders.
