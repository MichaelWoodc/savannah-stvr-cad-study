# Expanded individual-feature associations

Open index.html to browse the individual PNG/SVG charts. Every comparison has a logarithmic version and a regular linear version with whole-number tick labels. These are forest plots of rate ratios and 95% intervals, not box-and-whisker distributions. The reference is ratio 1, meaning no modeled association. Call-rate averages, such as 0.597 citizen calls per residential property/year, have different units and are not lines on these axes. Ratios and their intervals may be fractional even when axis labels are whole numbers.

Plots compare direct calls or property-plus-touching-neighbor call memberships, with advertised or approved guest capacity as alternative size controls. Pooled, owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied permit cohorts are evaluated separately. A missing cohort/outcome chart indicates insufficient supported models. All qualifying zero-call parcels stay in their model. No aggregate on-site amenity count is used.

Each feature is modeled separately with a Poisson model, controlling log(1 + capacity), log(1 + license count), and owner status in pooled models. Exposure uses the full available category period with a 59% focal rental booking scenario. Neighbors retain calendar exposure. Parking has shorter observed coverage and is not assigned zero calls for missing later months. Outcomes contain citizen-initiated calls only, with patrol/proactive exclusions. Source address/license matches remain audited. Maximum advertised capacity is a parcel-level proxy; feature presence may occur in another listing at the same parcel. These are not same-listing interaction tests.

Minimum support: 10 exposed and 10 comparison parcels, at least 3 events on each side, and at least 20 total events. Convergence and unstable-uncertainty checks also apply. Standard errors cluster spatial groups whose rental neighborhoods share parcels. Benjamini-Hochberg q values correct the full fitted comparison search across cohorts, capacity controls, call categories and direct/neighborhood scopes. Models do not remove location, reporting or other confounding. A high ratio does not establish causation, confirmed violations or rental guest responsibility.

The invitation/leisure keyword plots explicitly check bachelor (including batchelor), bachelorette (including batchelorette), engagement/proposal, wedding/bridal, pool table/billiards, swimming pool, hot tub and Jacuzzi. Event terms must occur with affirmative marketing language in the same sentence. Negated/prohibited and nearby/off-site sentences are excluded. This conservative heuristic can miss valid claims or misread complex wording; it is not a human-confirmed permission audit. Exact included and rejected excerpts, line numbers and source SHA256 are in keyword_source_evidence.csv. Zero qualifying engagement or pool-table evidence means no evidence passed this extraction, not proof that such properties do not exist. Unsupported requested keywords are displayed without estimated effects.

all_adjusted_associations.csv holds estimated models; insufficient_support.csv includes omitted and failed candidates. property_feature_call_audit.csv preserves all 484 matched properties, binary flags, capacities, URLs and call counts. This is an exploratory feature search, not a prediction contest or a ranking of confirmed offenders.

Rebuild from the project root: `python -m processing.expanded_associations`.

Display labels use plain amenity names and event names such as Bachelorette events. Positive/negative mention suffixes are omitted for physical amenities. Event indicators still require affirmative marketing and exclude prohibitions; shortening labels does not change the source criteria or model estimates. Both axes are titled Adjusted call-rate ratio; scale remains explicit in filenames and the plot browser.
