Thanks to its convenient location on the northern side of Savannah's Historic District, Douglass House is right next to countless nationally renowned attractions that are absolutely rich with cultural and historical value.

Oglethorpe Square



Oglethorpe Square is found on the second row of the five rows of squares in Savannah — namely, on the intersection of Abercorn Street and East President Street, where you'll be staying. As one of the six original colonial squares in Savannah — and one of the total 22 squares of Savannah — Oglethorpe Square features beautiful cobbled grounds, tons of shade thanks to its dozens of mature, and old yet well-kept buildings built in breathtaking Classical Revival and Georgian architectural style.



Located on the square's eastern side, the oldest building on Oglethorpe Square is the Owens–Thomas House. Today, the Owens–Thomas House takes the form of a historic house museum by Telfair Museums; Telfair Museums has its own history as being the very first public art museum to grace the Southeastern United States. The Owens–Thomas House, which was designated as a National Historic Landmark way, way back in 1976, is still considered one of the best examples of English Regency architecture in the entire country.



Renovations conducted on the Owens Thomas House in the 1990s found one of the best-preserved and oldest urban slave quarters in the Southeast United States. The renovations fixed the pantry and other areas of the kitchen quarters, which all feature strong hints of Gullah culture, an African American culture in the Lowcountry region that's almost extinct today.
